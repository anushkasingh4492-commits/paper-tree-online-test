# Unit 2: Complex Numbers and Quadratic Equations (all Class XI)

Official 2026 text: "Complex numbers as ordered pairs of reals, Representation of complex numbers
in the form a + ib and their representation in a plane, Argand diagram, algebra of complex numbers,
modulus and argument (or amplitude) of a complex number, Quadratic equations in real and complex
number systems and their solutions; Relations between roots and coefficients, nature of roots, the
formation of quadratic equations with given roots."

In scope (interpretation used for this bank):
- Ordered-pair definition, equality, a + ib, powers of i, conjugate and its properties, sum,
  product, quotient, multiplicative inverse, square roots of a complex number.
- Argand plane, modulus, argument and principal argument (always state $(-\pi, \pi]$), polar
  form $r(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)$, modulus/argument of products and quotients, triangle
  inequality, simple loci: $|z - z_1| = r$, $|z - z_1| = |z - z_2|$, $\operatorname{Re}$/$\operatorname{Im}$
  conditions, regions, distance between points, geometric meaning of $|z_1 - z_2|$.
- Quadratics with real or complex coefficients, solving in R and C, discriminant and nature of
  roots, conjugate-pair property for real coefficients, sum/product of roots, symmetric functions,
  transformed-root equations, formation from given roots, common root conditions, sign and range
  of a quadratic expression, max/min of a quadratic, conditions on location of roots (both
  positive, opposite signs, both greater than k, k between the roots), equations reducible to
  quadratics (in $x^2$, $|x|$, $a^x$, $x + \frac{1}{x}$), integral-root and parameter questions,
  recurrences $S_n = \alpha^n + \beta^n$ obtained from the quadratic itself.
- $\omega$ only as a root of $x^2 + x + 1 = 0$ (so $\omega^3 = 1$ follows); at most 4 items total.

Out of scope (do not write): De Moivre's theorem, nth roots of unity beyond the quadratic case,
rotation theorem / $\frac{z_3 - z_1}{z_2 - z_1}$ angle arguments, Euler $e^{i\theta}$ calculus,
cubic or higher polynomial theory (except reducible-to-quadratic), mathematical induction.
